A positioning concept for the Timescapes homepage.

This is an unsolicited redesign, built as a work sample for my application to the Senior Product Marketing Manager role in Vancouver.

The current homepage is very good at explaining what Timescapes is. My argument is that it should explain who its customer becomes. The buyer is a person with mud on their boots and a spreadsheet open on the other screen — the one who decided to put Timescapes on this project, and who gets the credit when the job runs better. They don't wake up wanting reality capture. They want the job to move faster, they want nobody hurt, and they want to not lose the argument at the end of it.

So the page is rebuilt around three words that customer already uses about their own work: faster, safer, smarter. Each is a link into the section that proves it.

Every metric, customer name and quotation below is drawn from timescapes.co as published. I have reframed existing copy and surfaced evidence that is currently buried — I have not invented a product claim anywhere on this page. The imagery, colour and typography are Timescapes' own, augmented with a single orange accent that marks what is new.

Build faster

Decisions at site speed, from wherever you happen to be standing.

  • Check deliveries, subcontractor activity and progress against programme without making a phone call or walking the site.
  • Arrive on site already knowing what you came for — fewer visits, and the ones you make are focused.
  • Assess real progress against your schedule before the weekly meeting, not during it.
  • Continuous automated capture replaces manual progress tracking entirely.
Auckland Airport · Hawkins

Cameras were placed for full coverage of a new domestic terminal inside a fully operational airport. Continuous capture removed manual tracking and let the team monitor progress without adding foot traffic to a live environment.

Source: Timescapes case study — Hawkins NZ / Auckland Airport
Their existing pillar was "Track progress from anywhere" — a feature name. The bullets are theirs, barely touched; only the frame changed. Note the third one: "before the weekly meeting, not during it" is the specific moment this product wins, and it is missing from the current site entirely.

Build safer

See the near miss, not just the incident report.

  • Review near misses and incidents in high-resolution footage, identify root cause, and put corrective measures in place before it happens twice.
  • Keep watch over live environments — an operating airport, a running rail corridor, an occupied building — without adding people to the hazard.
  • Coordinate daily planning with your crew off current site images, so everyone is working from the same picture of today.
Why this pillar exists

Auckland Airport asked Hawkins to use Timescapes, based on its track record on earlier airport projects. The owner specified the tool. That is the strongest commercial signal on the entire site — and it currently appears nowhere on the homepage.

Source: Timescapes case study — Hawkins NZ / Auckland Airport
This pillar is net new — there is no safety story on the homepage today, despite safety being named in Hawkins' testimonial and in the case study title. Every claim here is lifted from that case study; none is invented. If automated PPE and site-condition detection is far enough along to talk about, this is the section it belongs in, and it turns the pillar from evidence into a moat.

Build smarter

Win the argument before anyone starts having it.

  • Substantiate progress or delay claims with time-stamped visual evidence.
  • Confirm deliveries and installations were carried out correctly, the first time.
  • Overlay real site imagery against the 3D design model to catch clashes between what was drawn and what was built.
  • Give owners, investors and the public a single visual source of truth, rather than a version per stakeholder.
Glenbow Museum · EllisDon

A heritage transformation where design intent and physical build had to be reconciled continuously — the case where visual evidence and model overlay earn their keep.

Source: Timescapes case study — EllisDon / Glenbow Museum
Merges the current pillars 02 and 03. "The evidence you need, when you need it" is close to right already — it just needed to be about the person who does not lose the claim. The BIM clash line comes from the Hawkins study and is one of the most differentiated capabilities on the site, currently buried three clicks deep.

What I would validate before shipping any of this

  1. Whether "safer" survives contact with legal. Selling safety outcomes on a monitoring product invites questions about liability, and about surveillance of workers. I would pressure-test the pillar with a customer's H&S lead and with counsel before it goes anywhere near the hero.
  2. Whether the three words hold in all four markets. "Programme" versus "schedule", "site" versus "jobsite", and how NZ, AU, CA and US buyers rank the three outcomes against each other. That ranking should set the section order, and it may not be the same everywhere.
  3. Whether faster/safer/smarter matches how deals are actually won. This is built from public material only. Before committing I would read win/loss notes and sit on calls — if deals are consistently won on claims defence, "smarter" leads and the order changes.
  4. What the per-project sales motion needs from a homepage. Cameras deploy per project, so the growth engine is becoming the default across a contractor's whole portfolio. That is a different job from generating first-touch leads, and the site should be explicit about which one it is doing.
